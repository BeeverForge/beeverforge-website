# BeeverForge website

The production website at [beeverforge.com](https://beeverforge.com/) has complete static pages in English (`/`), Spanish (`/es/`) and Brazilian Portuguese (`/pt/`). The commercial journey uses two subscriptions, Prototype and Team, with a separate optional assisted start. Home, pricing, blog, trial, legal and technical documentation share the same navigation and approved vector identity. Visitors can switch between dark and light modes; the choice is stored locally.

## Build

Edit home and pricing content in `scripts/marketing-copy.mjs`, and their shared layout in `scripts/marketing.mjs`. Prices are defined once in the shared catalog. `final-preview/index.html` and `final-preview/pricing.html` are generated, non-indexed local previews; do not edit them. Trial content remains in `final-preview/try.html` and its translations in `scripts/translations.tsv`. Blog copy lives in `scripts/blog-content.mjs`; `scripts/blog.mjs` renders the index, linked guides and article structured data in the three languages. Run `node scripts/build.mjs` to generate all publishable pages, localized confirmation pages, retired-concept redirects and the sitemap. A local static server can serve the repository root for review. The source preview remains `noindex` and is not deployed.

The translation file has three tab-separated columns: exact English source text, Spanish, Portuguese. The architecture section (`/architecture/`) is built from `architecture-src/*.html` by `scripts/architecture.mjs`: each heading, paragraph, list item, table cell and diagram label is one row of the same file, code and elements marked `translate="no"` stay in English and appear in a row as `{0}`, `{1}`…, and every translation must carry the same tokens. Its source links are pinned to one core commit in `scripts/architecture.mjs`. Diagrams are Mermaid sources in `diagrams/*.mmd`, pre-rendered per language to `diagrams/svg/<name>.<locale>.svg`, which are committed and inlined by the build, so publishing needs no extra tool. After changing a diagram or one of its label rows, an author regenerates them with `PUPPETEER_EXECUTABLE_PATH=<a Chrome or Chromium binary> node scripts/diagrams.mjs [name…]`; it runs `@mermaid-js/mermaid-cli` at the exact version named in the script through `npx`, outside this repository, renders one diagram at a time and strips Mermaid's colours so `assets/docs.css` themes the result. In a label, text in backticks stays in English and is a `{0}` token in its translation row. The build keeps the same semantic page structure across languages. Shared scripts and styles live in `assets/`.

## The trial page and the legal pages

`/try/` (and `/es/try/`, `/pt/try/`) presents the Prototype Builder trial for companies and holds its waitlist form. The trial is a hosted company account in its trial plan: a company asks for it, BeeverForge approves it and sets up the company's own space at `<address>.beeverforge.com` with its own Prototype Builder, the person who asked becomes the account's admin and invites colleagues, the trial lasts fifteen days within the company's quota and never turns into a charge; when it ends, new work stops (except one generation of a prototype's requirements when the company asks BeeverForge's architects to look at it), and to continue the company subscribes to Prototype, the same account continuing at the same address with its data, and brings its own AI provider key; asking BeeverForge's architects to look at a prototype is free, and the assisted start is paid only if the company accepts their proposal (the pricing page says how). Its English source is `final-preview/try.html`; every text node is translated in `scripts/translations.tsv`, and the build fails on a missing one; the sentences the form says are attributes, in the `trial` table of `scripts/build.mjs`, and the build fails when a language lacks one. The form (`assets/try.js`) posts to the trial's door (`beeverforge-try`, `POST https://app.beeverforge.com/waitlist`) behind Cloudflare Turnstile. Besides the person's name, e-mail and description, it sends the company's name as `company` (1–120 characters) and the company's wished address as `slug`. The address field shows `.beeverforge.com` beside it and is checked in the browser by the rule the door and the infrastructure apply too (the hosted company accounts' contract): 3 to 20 characters, `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]{1,18}[a-z0-9]$`, no `--`, not ending in `-preview`, none of the reserved names (the list is in `assets/try.js`); a wrong address has its own sentence under the field, a malformed one and a reserved one each, and the form does not post until it is right. Once the person pauses on an address the rule takes (400 ms), the form asks the door whether it is free (`GET https://app.beeverforge.com/waitlist/address/<address>`; an address it answered is not asked again): taken — a company was opened there — has its own sentence and holds the form like a wrong address; free says `<address>.beeverforge.com is available.` under the field. The button pressed before the door answered asks at once and waits for the answer up to 1.5 s, asking in turn an address edited meanwhile. The door never tells another request's wish. When it does not answer, nothing is said and the form posts as before. The address is a wish the team may change, and the field's hint says so. The page's sign-in hint points to `https://app.beeverforge.com`. Its two settings live at the top of `scripts/build.mjs`: `waitlistEndpoint` and `turnstileSiteKey` (public). While the site key is empty, the form is read-only from the moment the page loads — the fields `readonly` and `aria-disabled`, the button disabled — and says the waitlist opens soon, with the contact address as a link; nothing is sent. The page carries its own Content-Security-Policy as a meta element, because GitHub Pages sends no headers.

The door's refusals each have their sentence: 403 asks to complete the check again, 503 says the check is not answering, 429 asks to wait (for the minutes in `Retry-After` when the door lets the browser read it), 400 and 422 ask to check the fields; anything else is the generic sentence. None of them says whether an e-mail address was already known.

**The laptop bed.** `BUILD_LOCAL=1 node scripts/build.mjs` also writes the local door (`http://127.0.0.1:8500/waitlist`) and Cloudflare's always-pass test key into the page, and adds the local door to the page's policy; served from `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`, the form then uses them. The default build (`node scripts/build.mjs`, the one the Pages workflow runs) carries neither, so the published page knows only `https://app.beeverforge.com`; served locally, its form is read-only from load, because the default build carries no local key.

The terms of use, the privacy policy and the acceptable use policy describe the trial as a company account (the hub at `app.beeverforge.com`, the company's own address, its admin and colleagues). They are Markdown in `content/legal/<en|es|pt>/`, rendered by the build to `/legal/<terms|privacy|acceptable-use>/` in each language. Each file starts with a front matter (`title`, `description`, `version`, `date`, `status`); `status: draft` shows the "under legal review" banner, publishes the page `noindex,follow` and keeps it out of the sitemap; `status: final` removes the banner, indexes the page and lists it in the sitemap once it is final in the three languages. A note for counsel is a bold bracketed segment, `**[… — to be completed by counsel]**`, and the build refuses a page that still carries one and is not a draft. Counsel's text replaces a file and nothing else changes.

The next version of a text, while it is drafted, lives in `content/legal/<en|es|pt>/next/<terms|privacy|acceptable-use>.md`, the same names in the three languages. The build publishes it at `/legal/next/<name>/`, always `noindex,follow` and out of the sitemap, with a banner that says it is not in force and links the version that is; the footer and the legal pages' own navigation keep linking the version in force only, and the build refuses a `next/` file whose status is not `draft`. Its notes say who must confirm each open point (counsel, an accountant or the product owner). When it is approved, it replaces `content/legal/<language>/<name>.md` with `status: final` and its version, the `next/` file is removed, and the door's `TRY_TERMS_VERSION` moves with the terms.

To review locally: `BUILD_LOCAL=1 node scripts/build.mjs && python3 -m http.server 8600 --bind 127.0.0.1`, then open `http://127.0.0.1:8600/try/`. With the door running on port 8500 (and `TRY_SITE_ORIGINS` naming `http://127.0.0.1:8600`), the form works end to end, and its states are to be checked against that door, not against mocked answers.

## The subscription and pricing journey

Home and `/pricing/` (including `/es/` and `/pt/`) show **Prototype at €19/month/company** and **Team at €299/month/company**, excluding applicable VAT. Team includes Prototype and replaces its subscription; a company does not pay both. Team includes the virtual Product Owner, orchestration and one execution at a time. The optional assisted start is requested from a concluded BeeverForge prototype; specialists analyze its requirements, design the architecture and prepare the scoped technical foundation. Its price, scope, acceptance and payment are agreed by proposal before work begins. Team coordinates subsequent implementation and evolution in the same company account; the service does not include or automatically activate Team. Its amount is not published on the website. Additional human work is separately quoted. Production requires human approval. AI, execution resources, app hosting and third-party services are paid separately by the customer. These two plans were reconfirmed by the owner on 2026-10-31 and replace the previous Business/Enterprise marketing comparison.

Online paid checkout is not open yet. All marketing pages and the trial page say this, using the availability sentence in `scripts/marketing-copy.mjs`. Team and assisted-start links go to the existing contact form, with the appropriate topic selected. The engineering core remains independently available under Apache-2.0, linked under Resources and in the footer. Specific deployment requirements can be discussed through the contact form; the website does not promise an additional plan or SLA.

The supplied 2m33s English walkthrough is served from `assets/beeverforge-overview.mp4`, with native video controls, `playsinline`, no autoplay and `preload="none"`. Its poster loads first, and a localized text summary is available alongside it. No external video player or font service is loaded. Dark is the default throughout the site, including when preference storage is unavailable. Light mode is used only when the visitor explicitly selects it; that choice is remembered.

Run `node scripts/build.mjs` and `node scripts/check-site.mjs` before publishing. The latter verifies generated sitemap pages, local destinations and fragment links, SEO metadata, shared offers and on-demand video. GitHub Pages runs both checks before deployment.

## Commercial positioning and editorial content

The homepage leads with the path from prototype to production and explains model choice, customer-owned API credentials, direct AI billing and customer-controlled execution infrastructure. Provider examples are options for configured deployments, not a claim that every model is validated or available in the hosted trial. The hosted trial is Anthropic-only today; beeverforge-try #93 remains open for broader paid-Prototype provider choice. Compatible private endpoints and local/on-premises models require deployment configuration, suitable hardware and validation. The public core and provider adapter were inspected at `ef33e7e4a59fa60e1fd6c8f906e0c0fa6e9bb0ee`.

The blog starts with two original guides, each localized into English, Spanish and Portuguese: the engineering journey from AI prototype to production, and model-agnostic delivery with customer credentials and infrastructure. Primary sources are linked in the articles. The CHI 2026 study supports the existence of prototype-to-product transition barriers in its sample. DORA and NIST supply delivery/security context. The RAND AI/ML study does not measure conversion of generated prototypes into businesses. No competitor conversion rate, guaranteed business outcome or fabricated success metric is published.

The assisted-start amount is omitted from home, pricing, metadata, trial FAQ and the nonbinding next-version legal draft. Existing legal documents in force and the billing catalog are not changed.

## Contact

The form uses the existing Cloudflare Worker endpoint and Resend configuration. The Worker secret is not stored in this repository. The public Worker accepts `beeverforge.com` and `www.beeverforge.com` origins; localhost is intentionally excluded. Local form tests must mock the network response. The visible confirmation page appears only after an HTTP success response. The English, Spanish and Portuguese forms share the same fields, validation and endpoint.

Google Analytics 4 uses measurement ID `G-JH25XRDJHG`. The tag loads only after a visitor accepts analytics cookies; visitors can reject or change their choice through Cookie settings in the footer. A successful project inquiry is counted as a `generate_lead` event only when analytics consent has been granted.

## Hosting

GitHub holds the code and GitHub Pages serves the static website through `.github/workflows/deploy-pages.yml` on `main`. Hostinger is the domain's registrar; its DNS is the Route 53 zone `beeverforge-infra` writes (`dns/`, since 2026-10-27), which keeps the apex and `www` pointed at GitHub Pages. The retired `/v1/` and `/v2/` routes redirect to `/` with `noindex`.
