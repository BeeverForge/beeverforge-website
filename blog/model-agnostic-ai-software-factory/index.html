Model choice & ownership

Your models, your credentials, your infrastructure: a model-agnostic software factory

BeeverForge editorial team · 31 October 2026

A software factory coordinates the work of building a product. It should let your company make deliberate choices about the AI behind that work, the credentials that pay for it and the infrastructure where it runs.

  4. Control the execution environment

Choose the engine for the work

Model choice is a product and operating decision: consider the task, quality, cost and data policy. BeeverForge separates orchestration from the configured coding engine. The platform can work with provider-backed models and compatible private endpoints, rather than making one model vendor the definition of your factory.

Make the provider choice explicit

Options in a configured delivery environment can include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Moonshot/Kimi and supported open-model services such as Qwen deployments. They are not interchangeable promises of quality. Choose a particular model, validate the tasks you need and agree on how failures are handled before relying on it for delivery.

Own the account that pays for AI

After the limited trial, your company supplies its own supported AI credentials and pays the provider directly. BeeverForge’s subscription pays for access to the platform. It does not buy a token bundle. Keeping provider accounts and usage budgets under your company’s control makes that cost visible independently of the platform fee.

Control the execution environment

The execution resources and applications run in infrastructure paid for by your company. A compatible local model can be served on premises when the model, API, hardware and security requirements fit. This is a deployment choice to configure and validate; it is not a claim that every component of the commercial platform runs offline by default.

Compatibility is more than a provider name

Kimi documents an OpenAI-compatible API. Ollama supports a subset of the OpenAI API for local-model use. Shared API shapes help integration, but do not guarantee identical behavior. Check the features required by the workflow, context limits, output quality and operating constraints with your actual model.

What no lock-in should mean

Look for choices you can exercise: which provider you pay, which model is configured and where the execution environment runs. Ask what configuration and validation a change needs. Model-agnostic does not mean zero dependencies, an automatic migration or a waiver of another provider’s terms. It means those dependencies are explicit and the architecture leaves room to change them.

The trial and the delivery environment

The hosted BeeverForge trial currently uses Anthropic with a limited budget. Broader provider choice belongs to the configured delivery environment and must be confirmed for that deployment. Discuss the required provider, private endpoint or on-premises model with our team before committing to a particular setup.
