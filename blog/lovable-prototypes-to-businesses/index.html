The central question

BeeverForge exists to take a validated idea to an operating product, with architecture, automated implementation and agreed delivery criteria.

182 apps with vulnerabilities in a sample of 200.

Deng, Fan and Meng’s study[1] audited deployed applications associated with Lovable and Claude Code. Researchers identified at least one vulnerability in 182 of the 200 evaluated apps — 91%. The work is available as a preprint, in its September 2026 version.

The sample was selected from applications with reachable deployments and public GitHub repositories, predominantly created by AI. The result describes the audited apps’ security. It does not measure abandonment or establish that 91% of all products from those tools fail.

For anyone turning an idea into an operating product, the finding raises a concrete question: what work continues after the application is already online?

Publishing is one stage. Operation needs continuity.

A demo impresses when the intended path works. A product must handle the day when two people book the same slot, an integration becomes unavailable or someone attempts to access another customer’s data. Those situations need architecture decisions, defined behavior and checks.

An internal system used every day is also an operating product. The criterion is whether it performs the agreed work: managing bookings, supporting a team, processing orders or connecting systems. Revenue, investment and commercial growth depend on other decisions; technical delivery must be verifiable in its own right.

Your idea should not end with a convincing demo and a list of technical tasks nobody has taken responsibility for.

The work continues even after publication.

An app can accept a booking while enforcing permissions incorrectly. Checking the intended experience is one part of delivery; testing access boundaries and failure responses requires dedicated verification.

A different method provides another piece of evidence: Veracode’s Fall 2026 update[2] found known vulnerabilities in code from roughly 38% of evaluated tasks. Its benchmark covered 18 models, four languages and four vulnerability categories. Results improved over the previous snapshot. They still support the need for verification; they do not attribute a 38% vulnerability rate to Lovable apps.

The transition from prototyping to maintenance is also discussed by CHI 2026 research[3], while DORA[4] examines verification and integration work. Our editorial reading: faster code generation becomes delivery when the work that follows has a process too.

Other tools face the challenge of completing and evolving software.

In an Aqua Voice practical comparison[9] conducted in October 2025, Base44, Replit, Bolt, Lovable, v0 and Rork were given one application scenario. In the published table, only one of the six tools passed all six evaluated features: authentication, posts, images, comments, likes and database. This is a single-case test of the versions available then, not a market-wide failure rate.

SlopCodeBench, preprint v2[10], focused on continuity: 15 coding agents were evaluated on 36 problems with requirements evolving through successive stages. No run passed every test at every stage of a problem end to end. This is a controlled code-evolution benchmark with strict criteria, not a measurement of Lovable or Bolt platforms in production.

These are different observations with a shared question for a buyer: how does the solution execute missing work, handle regressions and keep evolving when requirements change? That question belongs in platform selection from the start.

Lovable, prototypes and responsibility for delivery.

Lovable and similar tools have made experimenting with an idea much easier. That is valuable: testing an experience before committing to implementation can avoid months spent building the wrong product.

Lovable supports publishing and code export[7] and provides security checks[8]. The relevant comparison for BeeverForge is continuity of execution: who prepares the architecture, how requirements become implementable work and how changes proceed through checks to production approval.

Indexes such as VibeCodedIndex[6] help discover products but observe only part of the custom-domain landscape. Dividing the apps they discovered by Lovable’s total project count does not establish that the rest stalled. A useful comparison needs to distinguish created projects, observed deployments and products prepared for operation.

What needs to be ready when the product enters operation.

Return to the booking system. Validating the idea helps identify the steps a customer needs. Preparing operation means resolving booking conflicts, permissions, real data, payment failures and recovery. Each condition needs an owner and a way to confirm the result.

NIST’s Secure Software Development Framework[5] organizes secure development practices across the lifecycle. Applied to a specific project, these practices help turn “production ready” into criteria that can be assessed before delivery.

Sources and methodology

An article by the BeeverForge team. External findings are attributed and linked; the analysis and product positioning are ours.

  1. Deng, Fan & Meng · Understanding the (In)Security of Vibe-Coded Applications · preprint v4, 14 Sep 2026 ↗

    Preprint v4, 14 September 2026. Section 5: 182 of 200 audited deployed apps had vulnerabilities. Predominantly AI-created public GitHub sample associated with Lovable and Claude Code; not a platform-wide failure rate or research presented as peer reviewed.

  2. Veracode · Fall 2026 GenAI Code Security Update ↗

    Security-vendor benchmark: 18 models, four languages and four vulnerability categories, without security-specific guidance. Roughly 38% of tasks produced vulnerable code; not a study of complete apps or Lovable.

  3. CHI 2026 · From Throw-Away to Takeaway ↗

    Mixed-method study: 85 responses, 31 hackathon interviews and eight practitioner interviews. Examines post-prototype barriers; no population deployment rate.

  4. DORA · Balancing AI tensions · 10 Mar 2026 ↗

    Analysis of 1,110 open-ended responses from Google engineers about verification and integration, not Lovable project conversion.

  5. NIST · Secure Software Development Framework ↗

    Secure development lifecycle practices; not a BeeverForge certification.

  6. VibeCodedIndex · Lovable statistics · October 2026, live index ↗

    External commercial, live index of discovered custom domains. Partial coverage excluding lovable.app-only projects; no platform-wide production rate can be calculated.

  7. Lovable · Deployment, hosting, and ownership ↗

    Platform documentation used only to confirm hosting, export and portability features.

  8. Lovable · Security overview ↗

    Platform documentation used only to confirm available security checks.

  9. Aqua Voice · Vibe Coding Showdown · 30 Oct 2025 ↗

    External practical comparison: one scenario tested on 27 October 2025 across six platforms. Finding read from the six-feature table; not a population study or an evaluation of current versions.

  10. SlopCodeBench · preprint v2 · 7 May 2026 ↗

    Preprint v2, 7 May 2026: 15 agents, 36 problems and 196 checkpoints. Full success requires passing every test at every stage. Not a production-conversion rate for commercial platforms.
