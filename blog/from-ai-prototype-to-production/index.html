Production & delivery

From AI prototype to production: the work that makes a product real

BeeverForge editorial team · 31 October 2026

A prototype can make a business idea tangible in a conversation. The next question is whether the team can turn that shared understanding into software people can rely on. That is the journey BeeverForge is designed to connect.

  1. Start with a real business flow
  2. Carry the decisions into requirements
  3. Design the application that will actually run
  4. Make the release decision reviewable
  5. Plan the work after launch

Start with a real business flow

Consider the clinic example in our walkthrough. A check-in screen helps people agree on the experience. A real clinic also needs to decide who can change a record, how duplicate arrivals are handled and what happens when an external system is unavailable. These are product decisions that a convincing screen cannot settle on its own.

Carry the decisions into requirements

Use the prototype to discover rules and exceptions, then record them as requirements the team can evaluate. Name the intended users, permissions, data, acceptance criteria and unanswered questions. Keep these linked to the work as it changes. Otherwise each new stage has to reconstruct the intent behind the demo.

Design the application that will actually run

Agree on where the application runs, which systems it connects to and how sensitive data moves. Separate simulated data from real records. Decide how access, environments and configuration will work before calling the application ready. The right design is the one that fits the business and the agreed scope.

Make the release decision reviewable

A useful delivery path produces evidence: what changed, which configured checks ran, what an independent review found and what remains unresolved. In the BeeverForge engineering core, AI agents implement prepared work while project policies guide merging. A named person still authorizes production. The approval is a decision, not the automatic consequence of generating code.

Plan the work after launch

Identify who operates the application, how the team investigates problems and how changes return to the delivery flow. Agree on the required monitoring, backups and recovery for the particular project. These are delivery responsibilities to plan and validate, not unlimited managed services included in a platform fee.

What research can tell us

A CHI 2026 study (85 survey respondents; 31 hackathon and eight practitioner interviews) identifies post-prototype barriers for non-technical users. It does not establish a market-wide conversion rate. DORA’s 2025 research describes AI as amplifying an organization’s strengths and weaknesses. Our reading: delivery practices matter alongside generation tools. NIST’s SSDF likewise places secure development across the software lifecycle. Neither establishes a success rate for products created from AI prototypes. RAND’s cited AI/ML study excludes projects that simply use pretrained LLMs; its failure estimates are not a prototype-to-business conversion rate.
