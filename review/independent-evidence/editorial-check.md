# Editorial verification — 1 November 2026

The article leads with a scoped external observation of deployment, rather than monetization or an unsupported platform-wide failure rate. PT, EN and ES share the same source numbering and factual scope.

## Leading source

[Deng, Fan and Meng, Understanding the (In)Security of Vibe-Coded Applications, preprint v4, 14 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23130v4).

- Section 4 reports 1,007 validated, reachable deployment links among 9,041 public GitHub projects: 11.14%.
- Selection uses repository signals associated with Claude Code and Lovable and filters for predominant AI authorship. Private repositories are outside the corpus.
- A missing public link does not establish abandonment, technical failure or absence of private deployment. The article explicitly explains this in the opening section.
- Section 5 reports vulnerabilities in 182 of 200 audited deployed applications: 91%. This is a different denominator and outcome, kept separate from the deployment observation.
- The source is labelled a preprint, not asserted to have passed peer review. Its authors list independent, Microsoft and National University of Singapore affiliations. The findings were checked against the paper; we did not independently reproduce its dataset or audits.

## Corroborating and contextual sources

1. [Veracode, Fall 2026 update](https://www.veracode.com/blog/fall-2026-genai-code-security/): roughly 38% vulnerable task outputs across 18 models, four languages and four vulnerability categories, without security-specific guidance. This is a security vendor's controlled benchmark, not a Lovable production statistic. The article acknowledges improvement from the prior snapshot.
2. [CHI 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790757): post-prototype deployment and maintenance barriers in a mixed-method study, not a population conversion rate.
3. [DORA, 10 March 2026](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/): verification/integration work based on engineers' open responses, not a Lovable cohort.
4. [NIST SSDF](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/ssdf): lifecycle practice reference, not BeeverForge certification.
5. [VibeCodedIndex](https://vibecodedindex.com/lovable-statistics/): external but commercial and incomplete. Its discovered-domain counts are not divided by all Lovable projects to claim failure or production conversion.

Lovable documentation is retained only to confirm hosting/export and security features. Quantitative conclusions do not rely on Lovable marketing statistics.
